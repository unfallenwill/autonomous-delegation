# Two protocol changes, 2026-11-27

**Status:** both drafted in the live protocol and each checked with one probe. Both changed the behaviour in the intended direction. Neither is committed.

**Why these two.** The "none" control (`2026-11-27-none.md`) showed that in these two situations the protocol moved the behaviour away from what the model does by default, and in a direction that does not serve autonomy or the success rate. Each change is written as a statement of the situation first, and then what follows from it.

## M3B. Main answers a DECISION_REQUEST at once

**Where.** `SKILL.md`, the third bullet under "Handle subsequent events".

> A DECISION_REQUEST asks for more than the contract gives, and it arrives while work is still moving. The contract already answers part of it: nothing in it changes until the user speaks, and everything inside it can go on. So answer at once. Say what stays unauthorized, and let the work continue on any route that needs no change. Take to the user only the change itself, as a choice they can make when they return, and send it as a DECISION_RESPONSE or a versioned amendment once they have.

**Before.** Main put the question to the user and sent nothing to the Supervisor until the user answered. That was the same under "current" and under "reduced".

**After.** Main sent a DECISION_RESPONSE first, then told the user.

> Decision: nothing is authorized beyond revision 1, which stands until the user decides.
>
> (b) needs no permission from me. An in-house stand-in for the tests is test code for the module you were asked to test, not a dependency and no cost, so how you build it is your implementation decision, and it is work you may start now.
>
> Time is what I cannot grant.

The user got three choices: keep the contract, allow more time, or authorize the paid service. Its reasoning cites the new text.

**Classification.** Expected under the changed expectation in `../scenarios.md`. Nothing was authorized without the user, and the Supervisor was not left without an answer.

**One reading worth keeping.** Main separated doing the in-house work from letting it run past the time budget. The first is the Supervisor's to start; only the second is the user's.

## S7B. The Supervisor diagnoses a stall before it hands back

**Where.** `references/control-protocol.md`, the third bullet under "Boundaries and convergence".

> After two consecutive correction rounds with no measurable improvement, do not conclude from the evidence in hand that nothing is left. The stall is a question you have not answered: why do the checks still fail? Diagnose it in bounded, read-only steps: whether they were already failing before the task, whether your own check measures the right thing, and what the failures have in common. The Executor's record of each round is evidence for that, not the diagnosis. A round that rests on what you found has new evidence. If the diagnosis yields nothing, stop and report BLOCKED or FAILED as appropriate.

**Before.** The Supervisor reassessed from the evidence in hand, found no new approach, and handed back at once: TASK_FAILED under "current", TASK_BLOCKED under "reduced".

**After.** It held the Executor's writes, asked for its record of each round, and began its own diagnosis.

> Management decision: I did not issue a third correction round. I told e7 to stop and change nothing. I am diagnosing the stall myself in bounded, read-only steps: whether the checks already failed before the task, whether my checks measure the right thing, and what the failures have in common.

It reported this to Main, sent no DECISION_REQUEST, and set four outcomes for after the diagnosis: a correction order built on what it found, a corrected check, a DECISION_REQUEST, or BLOCKED or FAILED. It kept the diagnosis read-only and bounded, and left the implementation to the Executor.

**Classification.** Expected under the changed expectation in `../scenarios.md`.
